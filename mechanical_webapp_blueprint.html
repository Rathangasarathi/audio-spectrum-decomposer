§ 1 Problem Statement

Meriam & Kraige Problem 2/50 — A forearm of mass marm = 2.3 kg is held in position by the biceps muscle, which exerts a tension T at a perpendicular distance dT from the elbow pivot O. The forearm's centre of gravity is at point G, located dG along the arm from O. A sphere of mass ms is held at the hand at point A, a horizontal distance dA from O.

The student must:

  1. Determine the combined moment of the weights of the forearm and sphere about the elbow pivot O.
  2. Find the biceps tension T required for rotational equilibrium (ΣMO = 0).
Core Concept
This problem is a classic application of the scalar moment equation M = F × d. The forearm acts as a lever arm, and the biceps must produce a counter-clockwise moment to balance the clockwise moments of the two weights about O.

§ 3 Physics & Equations

Free-Body Diagram Description

The forearm is modelled as a rigid bar pinned at O (the elbow joint). Three forces act on it:

  • T — Biceps tension, acting vertically upward at the attachment point B, a horizontal distance dT from O.
  • Warm — Weight of the forearm, acting vertically downward at the CG point G.
  • Wsphere — Weight of the sphere, acting vertically downward at point A (hand).

The elbow joint provides pin reactions (Ox, Oy) but these do not create moments about O since their moment arm is zero.

T (↑) | upper | B G A arm O───┼───────●────────────────────────── ● sphere ▼ dT | dG along arm | pin ↓ W_arm ↓ W_sphere

Weight Calculations

Warm = marm × g      Wsphere = ms × g

Horizontal Moment Arms

Since moments about O use horizontal (perpendicular) distances for vertically-acting forces, the horizontal distance from O to G depends on the arm geometry:

xG = dG × cos(90° − θ) = dG × sin θ

Moment Equation (about O)

Taking counter-clockwise as positive:

ΣMO = T × dT − Warm × xG − Wsphere × dA = 0

Step 1: Combined Moment of Weights

Mweights = Warm × xG + Wsphere × dA    (clockwise about O)

Step 2: Required Biceps Tension

T = Mweights / dT    = (Warm × xG + Wsphere × dA) / dT
Key Insight
Because the biceps attachment is very close to the elbow joint (dT ≈ 50 mm) compared to the distances to the weights (dG ≈ 150 mm, dA ≈ 325 mm), the required biceps tension T is much larger than the combined weight — a classic mechanical disadvantage of the human forearm lever system.

§ 4 Sample Calculation (Default Values)

Step 1 — Compute weights
  W_arm    = 2.3 × 9.81 = 22.563 N
  W_sphere = 3.6 × 9.81 = 35.316 N

Step 2 — Horizontal distance O to G
  a = 90° − 55° = 35°   (angle of forearm below horizontal)
  x_G = 150 × cos(35°) = 150 × 0.8192 = 122.87 mm

Step 3 — Combined moment of weights about O (CW)
  M_weights = W_arm × x_G + W_sphere × d_A
            = 22.563 × 122.87 + 35.316 × 325
            = 2,772.04 + 11,477.70
            = 14,249.74 N·mm

Step 4 — Required biceps tension for equilibrium
  T × d_T = M_weights
  T = 14,249.74 / 50
  T ≈ 285.0 N

§ 5 Application User Flow

The interactive solver follows a streamlined five-step workflow:

① Load Page
→
② Enter Inputs
→
③ Diagram Updates
→
④ Compute
→
⑤ View Solution
  • Load Page: The solver loads with default values (θ = 55°, marm = 2.3 kg, etc.) and draws the initial diagram.
  • Enter Inputs: Seven input fields in the right panel allow the student to modify geometry, masses, and gravity. Supports both numeric and symbolic (letter) inputs.
  • Diagram Updates: As values are typed, the HTML5 Canvas redraws in real time — the forearm rotates to match θ, force arrows scale, and dimension labels update.
  • Compute: Clicking the gold "Compute Solution" button runs the solver.
  • View Solution: A results panel slides in with two answer boxes (combined moment and biceps tension T) followed by detailed step-by-step working.
Symbolic Input Support
If the student types a non-numeric value (e.g., "m" for mass), the app displays the symbol on the diagram and in the solution steps, while using the default numeric value internally for calculations and rendering.

Auto-Scaling Algorithm

The canvas diagram auto-scales based on the input values. The origin point O is positioned with fixed top/left padding, and a scale factor (pixels per mm) is computed to ensure the full arm + sphere fits within the available canvas area, accounting for reserve space for labels, arrows, and dimension lines.

Box Label Shows
Left Combined Moment of Weights Mweights in N·mm (clockwise about O)

Step-by-Step Working

Below the answer boxes, a detailed step-by-step breakdown is displayed using monospace font with colour-coded values. Each step shows the formula, numeric substitution, and result. The steps are:

  1. Compute weights — Warm and Wsphere from masses and gravity.
  2. Geometry — Calculate the forearm angle below horizontal (a = 90° − θ) and the horizontal distance from O to G.
  3. Moment of forearm weight — M₁ = Warm × xG.
  4. Moment of sphere weight — M₂ = Wsphere × dA.
  5. Combined moment — Mtotal = M₁ + M₂.
  6. Equilibrium — T = Mtotal / dT.

§ 10 Sign Convention

  • Positive x → right
  • Positive y → upward
  • Positive moment → Counter-Clockwise (CCW)
  • Weight forces act downward (negative y) — creating clockwise moments about O
  • Biceps tension acts upward (positive y) — creating a counter-clockwise moment about O

§ 11 Canvas Interaction Details

  • All inputs have input event listeners that call drawDiagram() on every keystroke.
  • The canvas uses devicePixelRatio scaling for crisp rendering on HiDPI displays.
  • The canvas resizes responsively on window resize via a ResizeObserver / resize event.
  • Force arrows, dimension lines, and labels are drawn programmatically — no bitmap assets required.
